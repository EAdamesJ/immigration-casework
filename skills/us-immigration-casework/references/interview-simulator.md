# Interview simulation

Offer practice in English or Spanish, preferably one question at a time. Ask whether the user wants a short or full practice. Use visitor.py interview to produce a queue tailored to missing/contradicted topics, then adapt follow-ups to the actual answer. The queue is a starting bank, not a prediction of a consular interview.

Explain the practice role briefly; do not impersonate a real government officer. Ask purpose, duration, payer/budget, occupation, return plans, travel history, U.S. contacts, refusals and petitions where relevant. Respect sensitive topics and permit skipping. After each answer, evaluate clarity, consistency with known facts and acknowledgment of uncertainty. Identify which document or fact could resolve uncertainty. Do not infer deception from voice, stress, expression, accent or response speed.

Keep original answers separate from suggested clearer wording. Suggest a concise truthful restatement only when the facts are supported. Do not supply a convenient date the applicant cannot remember. Never coach hiding a relative, petition, refusal or other material fact. An uncertain answer can be a cue to check records, not a reason to invent.

Finish with strengths, inconsistencies, unresolved facts, records to review, and a short practice plan. Do not announce approved/denied, issue a probability, or imply the simulation assesses legal admissibility. Voice practice is available only if the host actually supports it; the Python helper itself is text-only. Do not record or store transcripts by default; obtain consent and use private storage if requested.
