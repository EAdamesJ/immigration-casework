# Current sources and verification

## Source hierarchy

Identify issue, jurisdiction, agency, category, and relevant date. Check statute/regulation, binding judicial/administrative authority, agency policy, official instructions, and the case notice. Resolve conflicts by authority, scope, effective date, and applicable court orders; newer content is not automatically higher authority. Escalate unresolved legal conflicts for professional review.

For each material conclusion, retain source ID, title/type, URL or document/page, checked date, publication/effective date when available, relevant section, supported claim, and retrieval method. Distinguish direct retrieval, supplied copy, indexed excerpt, and historical document. Never say a blocked page was read directly. Reuse still-current retrieval.

Check redirects, hosts, content, and effective dates. These are starting points, not a statement that every route is currently accessible. Neither this package nor a matching domain verifies a legal interpretation. Local notices can be evidence without public URLs.

Reject lookalikes and proxy pages as substitutes for official authority. A search title saying USCIS does not authenticate its host. The bundled `source` command classifies a narrow allowlist only: an unlisted source can still be legitimate, requiring independent verification. It never fetches or dates a page.

## Volatile requirements

Recheck fees, editions, filing/payment methods, addresses, deadlines, fee-waiver rules, evidence requirements, interview instructions, income guidelines, visa availability, programs, officeholders, and agency organization before use. Never use fixed processing times/financial thresholds from a sample as current advice.

Use community reports to identify questions. Do not infer individual approval probability or a queue cutoff from self-selected anecdotes. If primary sources cannot settle an issue, explain the gap.
